## Waiver strategy — [FAAB or rolling priority]

*If rolling priority:* priority is a one-shot resource — win a claim and drop to
the back. Spend it only on a true difference-maker (a backup who just inherited a
job, a real breakout, a week-one starter for you). Otherwise submit nothing and
keep your priority; get everyone else off the **free-agent pool** (no cost).

*If FAAB:* budget the season. Bid aggressively on league-winners early when your
budget is full; save enough to compete for the one or two difference-makers that
appear midseason. Most weeks, use $0–low free-agent adds, not big bids.

- **[Waiver day] AM:** check every starter's status + next week's byes; submit only
  high-value claims.
- **Day after claims clear:** work the free-agent pool for streamers, bye fill-ins,
  and upside; drop your worst bench player — **never a handcuff to one of your studs**.
- **IR stash to claim on activation:** [player, earliest eligible week].

## Weekly routine (the part that wins the league)

- **[Waiver day] AM:** byes + injuries for every starter; submit waiver claims;
  drop worst bench.
- **Day after claims clear:** confirm claims; work the free-agent pool.
- **Saturday night:** set lineup by projection — no gut starts.
- **Sunday, ~90 min before early kickoffs:** check inactives for every questionable
  starter. Never leave an OUT/inactive player in the lineup.
- **Weeks 3–6:** sell high on any breakout to the managers who actually trade;
  cut obvious busts by ~Week 5.
